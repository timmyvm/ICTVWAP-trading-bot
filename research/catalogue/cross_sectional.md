# Catalogue family: cross-sectional factors and anomalies (S-XS)

Phase-0 cards for docs/CATALOGUE.md, compiled 2026-10-10. Outcome-blind: nothing here comes from this repo's
data or results. Every number is the source's own, for its market and era. "Read: full" = full text read (via
text extraction); "read: abstract" = abstract or listing page only; "recalled, not re-fetched" = memory, not
verified this session. The shared web-search budget ran out before every primary text was found; gaps are
flagged per card, not filled.

**Cost convention.** 13 bp round trip per unit traded. A $1-long/$1-short book that turns over fully costs
2 x 13 = 26 bp per rebalance. Perp funding is left out.

**Proposed P9 sub-tag (chair decides).** `P9-lottery`: leverage-constrained or lottery-seeking demand
overprices high-beta, high-volatility, high-skew assets. Shared by S-XS-06 to -09.

**Shared decay reference [MP16].** 97 US equity predictors: long-short returns 26% lower out of sample
(pre-publication) and 58% lower post-publication (monthly 0.582% in-sample, 0.402% out-of-sample, 0.264% post;
data to 2013). Bigger declines for predictors with high in-sample returns and for those in liquid,
low-idiosyncratic-risk stocks; valuation predictors decayed least.

### S-XS-01 Cross-sectional momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman J/K)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** JT93 (full), DM14 (abstract).
- **Rule:** Each month, rank stocks on their compounded J-month return (J = 3, 6, 9, 12). Form equal-weighted
  deciles: long the top, short the bottom. Hold K months (K = 3, 6, 9, 12) in overlapping cohorts, replacing
  1/K of the book each month. Variant B skips one week before entry. NYSE+AMEX, CRSP daily, 1965-1989.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary, P1 secondary (3-12 month trend). Wrapper: deciles, J/K, skip week,
  equal weights, cohorts. No stops, targets or filters.
- **Claimed mechanism:** Not systematic risk, not a lagged reaction to common factors. Part of the year-1 gain
  reverses in years 2-3, and the pattern repeats at earnings announcements: underreaction to firm news, with
  slow updaters on the other side. Limit to arbitrage: crash risk (DM14).
- **Published claim:** US 1965-89. 6/6: 0.95% a month (t=3.07), 1.10% with the skip week (t=3.61). Best cell
  12/3 with skip: 1.49% (t=4.28). Cumulative gain peaks at ~9.5% by month 12, ~4.1% by month 36. January
  -6.9% (t=-3.52); other months +1.66%.
- **Decay and crowding:** [MP16]. BHM11: this strategy made -8.5% a year in 2000-09, with a drawdown above 80%
  after 2000. DM14: crashes are partly predictable (after market falls with high volatility, during rebounds);
  volatility-managed exposure roughly doubles the Sharpe.
- **Cost-floor note:** At K=6, 1/6 of each leg turns over monthly, about 4.3 bp a month, against ~100 bp
  claimed. A weekly 1/1 crypto version turns over fully: 26 bp a week.
- **Event rate (estimate):** About 12/K independent spreads a year (2 at 6/6; 52 for weekly 1/1).
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here: perp daily closes, horizons in weeks.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Works in persistent trends. Crashes when the market rebounds hard after a
  high-volatility drawdown, because the loser leg is high-beta.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-03, -04, -05, -12, -13, -14: same P3+P1 trend, different ranking variable.

### S-XS-02 Short-term reversal (Lehmann weekly; Jegadeesh monthly)
- **Fidelity:** A for L90 (full text). The J90 monthly version is B (abstract only; forecast regression not read).
- **Sources:** L90 (full), J90 (abstract), N12 (abstract).
- **Rule (L90):** The week runs Wednesday to Tuesday. Weight w_i = -(r_i - r_ew) / (sum of positive
  deviations), using last week's return r_i and the equal-weighted market r_ew. That is $1 long last week's
  losers and $1 short its winners. Hold one week, then rebuild. Variant: rank on Wed-Mon returns, skipping
  Tuesday to blunt bid-ask bounce. NYSE/AMEX, 1962-07 to 1986-12. J90: forecast monthly returns from past
  returns (negative at lag 1, positive at lag 12) and sort deciles on the forecast.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P1 (1-week/1-month reversal) and P7 (liquidity provision) secondary.
  Wrapper: linear deviation weights, skip day.
- **Claimed mechanism:** L90: overreaction to fads. N12: reversal profit is the price of supplying liquidity
  and rises with VIX as suppliers withdraw. The other side is traders who need immediacy; it persists
  because liquidity capital is scarce in stress.
- **Published claim:** L90 (US 1962-86, gross): 1.79% a week on the full prior week, 1.21% with the skip day.
  Positive in ~93% of weeks and in all 49 half-years; roughly zero at 0.40% one-way cost. J90 (US 1934-87):
  extreme-decile spread 2.49% a month. N12: expected return and Sharpe jump with VIX, e.g. 2007-09.
- **Decay and crowding:** None measured in the sources read beyond N12's time variation; [MP16] applies.
- **Cost-floor note:** About 100% weekly turnover on both legs, so 26 bp a week. L90 broke even at 80 bp
  round trip. A daily crypto version pays 26 bp a day.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 a year weekly; 12 a year monthly.
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here: 1m-1d klines; skip-bar variant trivial.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Pays on deleveraging, liquidation and high-volatility days. Flat or negative in
  calm trends. In crypto it should appear mainly in illiquid names (S-XS-13).
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-13 (small-coin side), -15 (long horizon), -08 and -09 (partly reversal of extreme moves).

### S-XS-03 Residual (idiosyncratic) momentum
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** BHM11 (full).
- **Rule:** Each month, regress each stock's monthly return on Fama-French RMRF, SMB and HML over months t-36
  to t-1 (a full 36 months is required; the alpha is left out of the residual). Score = sum of residuals over
  t-12 to t-2, divided by their standard deviation over the same window. Equal-weighted deciles: long D10,
  short D1. Hold 1, 3, 6 or 12 months in overlapping cohorts. NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq common stocks, price at least
  $1; results 1930-2009.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary, P1 secondary. Wrapper: the factor hedge (residualizing), volatility
  scaling, deciles, hold.
- **Claimed mechanism:** Raw momentum carries large, time-varying factor bets (its beta flips negative after
  crashes). Removing them leaves stock-specific underreaction. Not a priced risk, not microstructure.
- **Published claim:** US 1930-2009, 1-month hold. Raw momentum: 10.26% a year, volatility 22.70%, Sharpe
  0.45. Residual: 11.20% a year, volatility 12.49%, Sharpe 0.90 (t=8.35). January: residual -0.32% (not
  significant) vs raw -2.60%.
- **Decay and crowding:** At least ~4.5% a year in every decade of the sample. 2000-09: +4.7% a year residual
  vs -8.5% raw. No post-publication data read.
- **Cost-floor note:** A 1-month hold means full turnover: 26 bp a month against ~93 bp claimed.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 12 a year.
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here, with unpublished adaptations: a crypto
  market factor (BTC or an index), no HML, weekly windows.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Beats raw momentum in rebounds and crashes, when the winners-minus-losers beta
  flips. No edge over raw momentum in a steady trend.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-01 (same primitive, hedged wrapper), S-XS-04.

### S-XS-04 Industry momentum
- **Fidelity:** B. **Sources:** MG99 (abstract), V25 (secondary, full). Rule details are secondary or recalled.
- **Rule (as V25 describes it):** Group stocks into industries by 2-digit SIC (20 industries in V25; not
  confirmed for MG99). Rank industries on past 6-month return. Long the best 3, short the worst 3. Hold 6
  months in overlapping cohorts. Value-weighted industry portfolios: recalled, not re-fetched.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary, P1 secondary, at group level. Wrapper: classification, 3-vs-3, 6/6.
- **Claimed mechanism:** The abstract states none. Slow diffusion of sector-wide news is the obvious
  candidate, but the source read does not say it.
- **Published claim:** MG99 abstract: industry momentum stays highly profitable after controls for size,
  book-to-market, stock momentum, dispersion and microstructure; stock momentum weakens sharply once industry
  momentum is controlled. No primary numbers read. V25's own 1963-95 replication: 0.89% a month (t=5.77).
- **Decay and crowding:** V25's extension to 2023 is lower, at 0.63% a month (secondary).
- **Cost-floor note:** At 6/6, 1/6 of the book turns over monthly: about 4.3 bp a month.
- **Event rate (estimate):** About 2 a year at 6/6. With ~10 crypto sectors, a 3-vs-3 sort is very thin.
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Partial here: it needs a coin-to-sector map
  (L1, DeFi, meme, AI...). The archive has none, and a hand-built map risks lookahead.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Works while sector narratives rotate slowly. Fails at narrative reversals and in
  market-wide crashes, when every sector moves together.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-01, S-XS-03.

### S-XS-05 52-week-high proximity
- **Fidelity:** B. **Sources:** GH04 (abstract), AA14 (secondary, full), Yu (full).
- **Rule:** Score = P(t-1) / High(t-1), where High is the highest price of the prior 52 weeks (AA14). Long
  the stocks nearest their high, short those furthest. NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq, 1963-2001. Top/bottom 30%, 6-month
  hold, one-month skip, equal weights: recalled, not re-fetched. The secondaries confirm only the 6-month
  horizon of the comparison strategies.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P8 (distance to a reference price) and P1 secondary. Wrapper:
  cutoffs, hold.
- **Claimed mechanism:** The abstract says only that the "readily available" high explains much of momentum.
  The anchoring story (traders underreact as price nears the reference high) is recalled, not re-fetched.
- **Published claim:** GH04 abstract: nearness to the high predicts better than past stock or industry
  returns; its forecasts do not reverse in the long run; short-term momentum and long-term reversal are
  separate effects. AA14: of the three strategies compared, the 52-week-high one "performs the best". No
  primary numbers read.
- **Decay and crowding:** Yu, 1962-2014 (author's numbers): the value-weighted spread is insignificant in
  month 1 (-15.94 bp, t=-1.48) while equal-weighted returns are positive. That points to small stocks.
- **Cost-floor note:** As S-XS-01 at a 6-month hold: about 4.3 bp a month.
- **Event rate (estimate):** About 2 a year at a 6-month hold; 52 in the weekly crypto version (S-XS-14).
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here (highs from klines), but needs a year of
  listing; S-XS-14 uses k-week highs instead.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Works while prices approach old highs without clearing them. Goes dead after a bear
  market, when nearly every coin is far from its high and dispersion collapses.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-14 (crypto version), S-XS-01.

### S-XS-06 Betting against beta (BAB)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** FP14 (full). Novy-Marx & Velikov critique: recalled, not re-fetched.
- **Rule:** beta = rho x sigma_i / sigma_m. sigma comes from 1-year daily log returns (at least 120 days); rho
  from 5-year overlapping 3-day log returns (at least 750 days). Shrink: 0.6 x beta + 0.4 x 1. Each month,
  split at the median beta, weight by distance from the mean rank, and lever each leg to beta 1:
  BAB = (r_L - rf)/beta_L - (r_H - rf)/beta_H. CRSP 1926-2012, plus 19 markets, bonds and futures.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P9-lottery (leverage demand) and P2 secondary. Wrapper: beta windows,
  shrinkage, rank weights, leverage to beta 1 (US: ~$1.4 long low-beta, ~$0.7 short high-beta).
- **Claimed mechanism:** Leverage-constrained investors (funds, individuals) buy high beta instead of
  levering, bidding it up. Unconstrained levered investors take the other side. The premium widens when
  funding is tight.
- **Published claim:** US: 0.70% a month (t=7.12), Sharpe ~0.78; 4-factor alpha 0.55% (t=5.59).
  International pooled: 0.64% (t=4.66). Treasuries: alpha 0.16% a month, Sharpe 0.81. Futures: positive in
  all four classes, but only equity indices significant. A higher TED spread goes with lower BAB returns.
- **Decay and crowding:** Recalled, not re-fetched: the critique says the returns depend on the beta
  estimator and on rank weights that overweight small illiquid stocks, and shrink after costs. [MP16].
- **Cost-floor note:** Beta ranks persist, so turnover is low. But gross exposure is ~2.1 per $1 of BAB, so
  costs (and funding on the levered perp long) scale with it.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 12 a year.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Equities, bonds and futures in the source. Y here, with beta against BTC or
  an index; the 5-year correlation window exceeds most perp histories. LTW22: crypto beta not significant.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Pays when leverage is cheap and retail chases high-beta coins. Fails in funding
  squeezes (margin hikes, funding spikes) that force the levered low-beta leg to cut.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-07, -08, -09 (P9-lottery cluster).

### S-XS-07 Idiosyncratic volatility (IVOL)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** AHXZ06 (full).
- **Rule:** Each month, IVOL = standard deviation of daily residuals from a Fama-French three-factor regression
  over the past month. No gap, one-month hold (1/0/1). Value-weighted quintiles: long Q1 (low IVOL), short Q5.
  NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq (exact filters not extracted), 1960s-2000.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P2 and P9-lottery secondary. Wrapper: window, quintiles.
- **Claimed mechanism:** None established. The authors test and reject liquidity, size, value, volume,
  coskewness, dispersion and momentum: "something of a puzzle". BCW11 report that adding MAX reverses it.
- **Published claim:** Q5-Q1 raw: -1.06% a month; FF3 alpha -1.31% (t=-7.00). Robustness: 1/1/1 -0.82%
  (t=-4.63); 12/1/12 -0.77%; NYSE-only -0.66% (t=-4.85).
- **Decay and crowding:** [MP16]. Crypto: LTW22 find IVOL not significant, 2014-18.
- **Cost-floor note:** IVOL ranks persist, so turnover is partial: at most 26 bp a month against ~106 bp claimed.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 12 a year (52 if weekly).
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here: residuals against a crypto market factor.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Lottery demand peaks in retail alt seasons, which is exactly when the high-IVOL short
  leg is most exposed to squeezes. Expect the sign to flip in mania months.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-06, -08, -09.

### S-XS-08 MAX (lottery demand)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** BCW11 (full).
- **Rule:** MAX = a stock's largest daily return in month t-1; MAX(N) = mean of its N largest, N = 1 to 5.
  Deciles, value- or equal-weighted, held through month t: long D1, short D10. NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq, no price
  filter; tables cover 1962-07 to 2005-12.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P9-lottery and P1 secondary (reversal of an extreme day). Wrapper:
  deciles, N, weighting.
- **Claimed mechanism:** Poorly diversified, lottery-seeking investors overpay for small chances of big gains
  (cumulative prospect theory). Short-sale limits and costs keep it alive. Expectations are not measured.
- **Published claim:** Value-weighted D10-D1: -1.03% a month (Newey-West t=-2.83); 4-factor alpha -1.18%
  (t=-4.71). Equal-weighted: -0.65% (t=-1.83). High-MAX stocks are small, illiquid, low-priced (median $6.47)
  and high-beta, with weak prior 11-month returns.
- **Decay and crowding:** [MP16]. Crypto: LTW22 find maximum daily return not significant, 2014-18.
- **Cost-floor note:** Only 35% of the top decile stays there the next month, so the short leg churns. At
  most 26 bp a month against ~103 bp claimed (value-weighted).
- **Event rate (estimate):** 12 a year.
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here: 1d klines; intraday-max variants are
  possible but unpublished.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Strongest when lottery demand is high and shorting is costly. Perps have no borrow
  constraint, so if short-sale limits drive the effect it should be weaker in perps than in spot.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-07, -09, -02.

### S-XS-09 Realized skewness (weekly, intraday)
- **Fidelity:** B. **Sources:** ACJV15 (abstract only; full rule not read).
- **Rule:** Each week, compute each stock's realized skewness from intraday returns over the week. Deciles:
  long the lowest-skew decile, short the highest; hold the next week. Not specified in what was read: the
  intraday interval, the weighting and the week boundaries.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P9-lottery and P2 secondary. Wrapper: deciles, sampling interval.
- **Claimed mechanism:** The abstract gives none. "Positive skew is overpriced" is the usual reading, an
  inference rather than the source's claim.
- **Published claim:** About 19 bp the next week (t=3.70), not explained by the Fama-French or Carhart
  factors. Realized kurtosis is positive but not consistently significant; realized volatility shows no
  strong relation.
- **Decay and crowding:** Not read. LTW22 find crypto skewness not significant, but from daily returns, a
  different estimator.
- **Cost-floor note:** Full weekly turnover costs 26 bp a week against 19 bp claimed: below the floor before
  any funding.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 a year.
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here: 1m klines give weekly realized moments.
- **Regime hypothesis:** May partly be the reversal of weekly jumps (big up-jump, high skew, fade). If so, it
  should move with S-XS-02's liquidity regime.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-08, S-XS-02.

### S-XS-10 Amihud illiquidity
- **Fidelity:** A for the measure. The published test is a regression, not a trading rule; a sort would be an
  adaptation. **Sources:** A02 (full).
- **Rule:** ILLIQ = yearly mean of |daily return| / daily dollar volume (x10^6), divided by that year's
  cross-sectional mean (ILLIQMA). Filters: NYSE, more than 200 days of data, price above $5, top and bottom 1%
  of ILLIQ dropped. Test: monthly Fama-MacBeth regressions of year-y returns on ILLIQMA from year y-1, with
  controls.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary, P7 secondary. Wrapper: filters, annual window, mean adjustment.
- **Claimed mechanism:** Compensation for expected cost and price impact. Flight to liquidity: unexpected
  illiquidity lowers prices at the time it happens.
- **Published claim:** US 1964-97: ILLIQMA coefficient 0.162 (t=6.55), positive in 63.4% of months; 0.126
  (t=5.30) excluding January. Expected market illiquidity raises excess returns; unexpected illiquidity lowers
  them. Both effects are stronger in small firms.
- **Decay and crowding:** [MP16] (hard-to-arbitrage predictors decayed less). Crypto: LTW22 Amihud spread
  2.6% a week (t=1.48, not significant). BK19: an illiquid-minus-liquid premium only among 14-day losers
  (0.52% a day).
- **Cost-floor note:** The long leg is the costliest to trade by construction. Its real spreads are likely
  many times 13 bp, so the 13 bp assumption fails for that leg (arithmetic, not data).
- **Event rate (estimate):** 12 a year in the source; 52 or more in crypto.
- **Applies to / testable here:** US equities in the source. Y here: |r| / quote volume. Perps span a
  narrower liquidity range than the spot universes in the crypto papers.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Earns in calm, loses in liquidity shocks: holding it is effectively short a
  liquidity crisis.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-11, -12 (dollar volume), -13.

### S-XS-11 Size (small minus big)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** B81 (full), AFIMP18 (full).
- **Rule:** B81's "arbitrage" portfolio holds the n = 10, 20 or 50 smallest NYSE firms long and the n largest
  short, equal-weighted. It is formed every 5 years, rebalanced monthly and beta-adjusted (the intercept on
  market excess return). Modern SMB (AFIMP18 description): a 2 size x 3 book-to-market sort, the mean of the
  3 small portfolios minus the mean of the 3 big.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary, P7 secondary (size proxies liquidity). Wrapper: n, rebalance
  interval, beta adjustment.
- **Claimed mechanism:** B81: unknown, perhaps a proxy for "true unknown factors". AFIMP18: small firms tend
  to be junk, so plain SMB is short quality, which hides the premium.
- **Published claim:** B81, 1931-75: smallest-10 minus largest-10 earns 1.52% a month beta-adjusted (t=2.99);
  n=50 earns 1.01%. Nonlinear: small vs median 1.30%, median vs large 0.21% (not significant).
  AFIMP18, US 1957-2012: SMB alpha 14 bp a month (t=1.23), 49 bp (t=4.89) with a quality control; quality
  control raised the alpha in 23 of 24 international regions.
- **Decay and crowding:** AFIMP18: the premium "largely disappeared" in the early 1980s and 1990s, after B81
  (1980-99 SMB alpha ~-11 bp), and returned after 2000. Crypto: BLTW25 CSIZE weaker after 2020 (S-XS-12).
- **Cost-floor note:** Size ranks barely move, so turnover is minimal; the cost sits in the small leg.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 12 a year nominally; highly persistent, so few independent events.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Equities in the source; crypto in S-XS-12. Partial or N here: the archive
  has no market cap or supply data (volume and OI measure liquidity, not size), and perps exclude micro caps.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Works in risk-on phases with abundant liquidity; fails in flight to quality.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-10, S-XS-12.

### S-XS-12 Crypto factors: size, momentum, volume (Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** LTW22 (full), BLTW25 (full, v3).
- **Rule:** Weekly returns from daily closes. Universe: coins above $1M cap on CoinMarketCap, dead coins
  included; 1,707 coins, 2014-2018. CMOM: each week, sort on the past 3-week return, 30/40/30, value-weighted,
  top minus bottom, held one week. CSMB: the same split on log market cap at the end of the formation week.
  Single-characteristic tests use value-weighted quintiles, Q5-Q1.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P1 (momentum) and P7 (size, volume) secondary. Wrapper: cutoffs,
  value weighting, the $1M floor, and the 3-week lookback (chosen in-sample for the biggest spread).
- **Claimed mechanism:** Little offered: small coins are illiquid; momentum loadings are "transient". No cost
  analysis. Shorting BTC instead of the loser quintile kills significance at 1, 2 and 4 weeks (3 weeks: 3.0%).
- **Published claim:** LTW22, weekly quintile spreads. Momentum: 1 week 2.7% (t=1.99), 3 weeks 4.1% (t=2.74),
  4 weeks 2.5% (t=2.00); 8-100 weeks not significant. Market cap -3.4% (t=-2.56); dollar volume -3.2%
  (t=-2.02). Momentum is 4.2% a week above the median cap and 0.6% (n.s.) below it. Not significant: beta,
  IVOL, skewness, max daily return, Amihud, age. BLTW25 (2013-12 to 2025-09, 16,468 coins): CMOM (2-week)
  2.6% a week (t=3.89); CSIZE -2.3% (t=-2.66).
- **Decay and crowding:** BLTW25 after 2020 (out of sample for the 2019 working paper): momentum 2.1% a week
  (t=3.70); size -0.9% (t=-1.69).
- **Cost-floor note:** Up to 26 bp a week against claimed 2-4% a week, but measured on universes far wider
  than the perp list.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 a year nominally; one market state drives each week, so perhaps 20-50 effective.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Crypto spot in the source. Momentum and volume: Y. Size: partial (no cap).
- **Regime hypothesis:** Momentum: attention-driven rotation among large coins; fails after blow-off tops.
  Size: risk-on inflows to small coins.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-01, -10, -11, -13.

### S-XS-13 Crypto momentum in large coins, reversal in small/illiquid ones
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** F23 (full), BK19 (full), D22 (secondary).
- **Rule (F23):** Weekly; CoinMarketCap coins of at least $1M cap, excluding stablecoins and wrapped coins.
  Using previous-week values, a coin is Large&Liquid if its cap is at least $50M and weekly volume at least $5M,
  otherwise Small/Illiquid. Within each group, sort on the sum of log weekly returns over h = 1, 2, 4, 12 or 26
  weeks into equal-weighted quintiles (returns winsorized at 1000%). Large&Liquid: long Q5, short Q1.
  Small/Illiquid: the reverse. Hold one week. Sample 2017-06 to 2022-12.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P1 and P7 secondary (the liquidity state flips the sign of P1).
  Wrapper: thresholds, quintiles, equal weights.
- **Claimed mechanism:** BK19: herding in liquid coins. Reversal in illiquid coins looks like a liquidity
  reward; F23 finds it is driven mainly by low volume. F23 warns that trading at weekly closes may not be
  executable.
- **Published claim:** F23 (weekly). Large&Liquid: 1 week 1.36% (t=2.33), 2 weeks 1.44% (t=2.69), 4 weeks
  0.84% (t=1.57); the 1-week BTC-CAPM alpha is not significant. Small/Illiquid: 1 week -3.91% (t=-7.31),
  negative at all horizons, concentrated in coins under $100k weekly volume. BK19 (2015-19, 711 coins, 14-day
  formation and hold, 30/40/30 x Amihud): liquid UMD 0.26% a day, illiquid UMD -0.33% a day. D22 (secondary):
  momentum strongest at a 2-week sort and hold, reversal from ~4 weeks.
- **Decay and crowding:** None beyond S-XS-12's post-2020 momentum persistence.
- **Cost-floor note:** 26 bp a week against the large-coin claim of 136-144 bp. The small-coin reversal leg
  pays spreads far above 13 bp and is mostly not listed as perps.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 a year nominally; 20-50 effective.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Crypto spot in the source. Y for the large/liquid side (where most perps
  sit); mostly N for the micro-cap reversal side.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Inside perps, the sign of short-horizon P1 should depend on volume rank: momentum in
  the top volume tercile, reversal in the bottom.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-12, -02, -14.

### S-XS-14 Crypto distance to the k-week high ("high momentum")
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** F23 (full); it adapts GH04.
- **Rule:** Score_h = ln(close of week t) - ln(highest intraday price over the past h weeks), h = 1, 2, 4, 12
  or 26. Universe, liquidity split, equal-weighted quintiles and one-week hold as in S-XS-13. Large&Liquid:
  long Q5 (nearest the high), short Q1. Small/Illiquid: the reverse.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary; P8 (distance to a price level) and P1 secondary. Wrapper: split,
  quintiles, h.
- **Claimed mechanism:** GH04's anchoring story, borrowed and not tested.
- **Published claim:** 2017-06 to 2022-12, weekly. Large&Liquid: h=1 2.68% (t=4.93), h=4 2.60% (t=4.75),
  h=12 1.00% (t=1.94), h=26 0.57% (t=1.19). The 3-factor alpha (BTC, size, 1-week momentum) is significant
  (t=5.72). Small/Illiquid: h=1 -3.67% (t=-9.03); 3-factor alpha -2.43% a week (t=-4.37).
- **Decay and crowding:** None: a single working paper.
- **Cost-floor note:** 26 bp a week against ~260 bp claimed (Large&Liquid).
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 a year nominally; 20-50 effective.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Crypto spot in the source. Y here: intraday highs from klines.
- **Regime hypothesis:** As S-XS-05. Also a candidate state variable for S-XS-13: closeness to a recent high
  may separate continuation from exhaustion.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-05 (equity original), S-XS-13 (same split, different ranking variable).

### S-XS-15 Crypto "value" as long-horizon reversal (negative 52-week return)
- **Fidelity:** A. **Sources:** CKTZ22 (full). The value definition follows Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen
  (2013) as cited there; that paper was not read.
- **Rule:** Each week, sort coins independently into 2 size groups (50/50) and 3 groups (30/40/30) on
  NPast52, minus the past 52-week return. Value-weighted portfolios. VAL = mean(high-value small, high-value
  big) - mean(low-value small, low-value big). Weekly rebalance. Universe: 4,007 tokens on CoinMarketCap,
  2014-01 to 2021-01, excluding stablecoins and rows with zero price, cap or volume.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P3 primary, P1 secondary (52-week reversal). Wrapper: 2x3 sort, value weights,
  window.
- **Claimed mechanism:** Coins as hybrid assets, so a commodity/currency-style value premium might apply.
  Not tested. Fundamental ratios (transactions, addresses or users to market cap) gave no significant spread.
- **Published claim:** VAL 4.00% a week (t=5.63). The spread falls with size: 15.1% a week in small coins,
  0.8% in big ones; in a large-cap sample 1.7% (t=1.44, not significant). SMB 5.18% a week (t=4.57).
- **Decay and crowding:** Indirect: BLTW25 find 12- and 24-month momentum spreads insignificant through
  2025, using a different single-sort design.
- **Cost-floor note:** 52-week ranks barely move week to week, so the cost is well under 26 bp a week.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 rebalances a year, but the overlapping signal gives fewer than 10
  independent events a year.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Crypto spot in the source. Y in principle, but it needs 52 weeks of history
  (many perps lack it), and delisted "zombie" bars must be screened out (a known archive issue).
- **Regime hypothesis:** Works after bubbles deflate, when the deepest losers rebound relative to the rest.
  Fails when the losers are dying projects that delist, so it is survivorship-sensitive.
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-02 (short-horizon reversal), S-XS-13; the inverse of a long-lookback S-XS-01.

### S-XS-16 Crypto network adoption and price-to-new-address
- **Fidelity:** B. Full texts read, but the exact on-chain definitions were not extracted.
- **Sources:** CKTZ22 (full), BLTW25 (full, v3).
- **Rule:** NET (CKTZ22): on the core sample of 616 coins with IntoTheBlock on-chain data, sort each week
  30/40/30 on growth in addresses with a balance; top minus bottom. The growth window and weighting were not
  extracted. CVALUE (BLTW25): each week, sort coins above $1M cap into value-weighted quintiles on
  price-to-new-address ratio, take Q5-Q1, hold one week. The new-address window was not extracted.
- **Primitive vs wrapper:** P9 primary (on-chain adoption fundamentals; a candidate new primitive), P3
  secondary. Wrapper: cutoffs, weighting.
- **Claimed mechanism:** Network effects: value comes from the user base (CKTZ22 cite Cong, Li & Wang 2021).
  Not formally tested.
- **Published claim:** NET 3.76% a week (t=2.82), 2014-2021. CVALUE Q5-Q1 -3.5% a week (t=-3.50), full
  2013-2025 sample: coins that are cheap per new address outperform.
- **Decay and crowding:** CVALUE after 2020: -2.3% a week (t=-2.32), weaker but still significant.
- **Cost-floor note:** Adoption ratios move slowly, so turnover is low. Not binding, on arithmetic.
- **Event rate (estimate):** 52 a year nominally; the effective count is unknown.
- **Applies to / testable here:** Crypto spot in the source. N here: it needs per-coin on-chain address data,
  which the archive does not have.
- **Regime hypothesis:** Works in adoption-growth phases. Fails when address counts are gamed (airdrop
  farming, spam addresses).
- **Overlaps:** S-XS-15 (crypto value), S-XS-11 (small chains).

## Family notes

These 16 cards hold about four distinct primitives. (1) Relative trend, P3+P1: S-XS-01, -03, -04, -05, -12,
-13 large-coin side and -14. These differ only in the ranking variable (raw return, residual, industry,
distance to high) and the horizon. (2) Relative reversal / liquidity provision, P3+P1+P7: S-XS-02, the
small-coin side of -13, and -15 at a long horizon. (3) Lottery/leverage demand, P9-lottery: S-XS-06 to -09.
(4) Size/illiquidity premium, P3+P7: S-XS-10, -11 and the size leg of -12. S-XS-16 adds on-chain
fundamentals, which cannot be tested here. No card is unmechanizable, but -04 and -05 rest partly on recalled
parameters. In crypto, cluster 3 is mostly insignificant, and the sign of short-horizon P1 flips with
liquidity.
